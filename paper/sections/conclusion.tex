\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method, a bilevel optimization framework for LLM unlearning that treats retain preservation as a hard constraint using an augmented Lagrangian.
The method has two key components: (1) a clamped entropy forget loss that gives bounded, self-stabilizing forgetting with per-token dropout, and (2) an asymmetric dual update that calibrates the forget--retain tradeoff from training dynamics instead of manual tuning.
All updates happen in LoRA adapters, which limits how much unlearning can damage other weights.

On TOFU across three forget splits, two model scales, and 5 seeds, \method outperforms all baselines on the majority of settings. The largest gains are on small forget sets (forget01 1B: $+$0.118 HM over PDU).
LLM judge evaluation confirms near-zero forget leakage while baselines still retain substantial residual knowledge.
On MUSE Books, \method achieves HM=0.798, the highest among all tested methods.
The three-phase loss dynamics are consistent across settings, which suggests the ALM converges to a stable operating point for the forget--retain tradeoff.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our evaluation covers TOFU and MUSE Books. Extending to WMDP (safety-critical unlearning) and larger instruction-tuned models ($\geq$7B) is left for future work.
MUSE Books is a single-seed result and we plan to add multi-seed evaluation.
\method has more hyperparameters than single-loss baselines, although auto-epsilon reduces the tuning effort.

\paragraph{Future work.}
We plan to extend to additional benchmarks (MUSE News, WMDP), run a formal ablation study to isolate each component's contribution, and scale to 7B+ instruction-tuned models.
