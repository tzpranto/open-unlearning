\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

We evaluate on four benchmarks that test different facets of LLM unlearning:

\paragraph{TOFU} \citep{maini2024tofu} contains 200 fictitious author profiles with 20 QA pairs each (4000 total). We test on three forget splits: forget01 (1\%), forget05 (5\%), and forget10 (10\%). All TOFU results are averaged over 5 random seeds. Key metrics: Model Utility (MU, harmonic mean of 9 retain/real-authors/world-facts sub-metrics), forget answer probability (Prob), and forget ROUGE-L (ROUGE). Overall score: $\text{HM} = \text{hmean}(\text{MU},\; 1{-}\text{Prob},\; 1{-}\text{ROUGE})$.

\paragraph{MUSE Books} \citep{shi2025muse} targets removal of Harry Potter book content from Llama-2-7B. The forget set is a single cohesive narrative (554 samples). Key metrics: forget knowledge (fk), verbatim memorization (vm), retain knowledge (rk). Overall score: $\text{HM} = \text{hmean}(1{-}\text{fk},\; 1{-}\text{vm},\; \text{rk})$.

\paragraph{MUSE News} \citep{shi2025muse} targets removal of BBC news articles from Llama-2-7B. Unlike Books, the forget set consists of 889 short factual articles---a distributed multi-document setting where knowledge is spread across many independent documents. This presents a fundamentally different challenge: the model must forget heterogeneous, independently memorized content rather than a single cohesive text. Same metrics as MUSE Books.

\paragraph{KnowUndo} \citep{tian2024knowundo} evaluates unlearning on Llama-2-7B-chat across two domains: \emph{copyright} (removing copyrighted book content) and \emph{privacy} (removing personal information). Unlike the other benchmarks, KnowUndo starts from a LoRA-fine-tuned target model rather than a fully fine-tuned one. Key metrics: forget ROUGE-L (fgt\_R), retain ROUGE-L (ret\_R), and MMLU. Overall score: $\text{HM} = \text{hmean}(1{-}\text{fgt\_R},\; \text{ret\_R},\; \text{MMLU})$.

\paragraph{LLM Judge.} We evaluate all methods using an LLM judge (Claude Opus~4.7) that scores model outputs on three axes (0--2 scale): Forget Leakage (FL, $\downarrow$), Retain Accuracy (RA, $\uparrow$), and retain Response Quality (rRQ, $\uparrow$). Overall: $\text{HM}_\text{J} = \text{hmean}(1{-}\text{FL}/2,\; \text{RA}/2,\; \text{rRQ}/2)$. This captures generation quality beyond token-level statistics.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against seven baselines, all using their published default hyperparameters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GradAscent} \citep{jang2023knowledge}: maximize CE on forget data.
    \item \textbf{GradDiff} \citep{yao2024large}: weighted sum of forget GA and retain CE.
    \item \textbf{NPO} \citep{zhang2024negative}: DPO-style preference optimization against memorized outputs.
    \item \textbf{SimNPO} \citep{fan2025simplicity}: reference-free variant of NPO.
    \item \textbf{BLURNPO} \citep{reisizadeh2026blur}: bilevel formulation with gradient projection.
    \item \textbf{RMU} \citep{li2024wmdp}: representation misdirection toward random targets.
    \item \textbf{PDU} \citep{entesari2025constrained}: primal-dual constrained optimization with logit-margin loss.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:implementation}

\paragraph{MUSE Books/News (Llama-2-7B).}
LoRA $r\!=\!16$, $\alpha\!=\!32$ on all projections (q/k/v/o, gate/up/down).
$K\!=\!3$, $\eta_{\text{in}}\!=\!2\text{e-}4$, $\eta_{\text{out}}\!=\!3\text{e-}5$, $\varepsilon\!=\!0.15$, $\rho\!=\!0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{init}}\!=\!1.0$, $\tau\!=\!0.7$.
Batch size 2, gradient accumulation 8 (effective batch size 16). 4 epochs.

\paragraph{TOFU (Llama-3.2-1B/3B).}
LoRA $r\!=\!8$, $\alpha\!=\!16$.
$K\!=\!3$, $\eta_{\text{in}}\!=\!2\text{e-}4$, $\eta_{\text{out}}\!=\!2\text{e-}5$, $\varepsilon_{\text{mul}}\!=\!0.85$, $\rho\!=\!0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{init}}\!=\!1.0$, $\tau\!=\!0.7$.
Batch size 4, gradient accumulation 4 (effective batch size 16). $T\!=\!100$ steps.

\paragraph{KnowUndo (Llama-2-7B-chat).}
LoRA $r\!=\!16$, $\alpha\!=\!32$.
$K\!=\!3$, $\eta_{\text{in}}\!=\!2\text{e-}4$, $\eta_{\text{out}}\!=\!3\text{e-}5$, $\varepsilon_{\text{mul}}\!=\!0.85$, $\rho\!=\!0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{init}}\!=\!1.0$, $\tau\!=\!0.7$.
Batch size 2, gradient accumulation 8 (effective batch size 16). 4 epochs.
The target model is a LoRA-fine-tuned checkpoint; we initialize our LoRA adapters from the target model weights and unlearn on top of them.

All experiments run on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU (80GB).

The ablation reveals a clear hierarchy of component importance:

\paragraph{ALM constraint (A16).} Removing the augmented Lagrangian ($\lambda\!=\!0$, $\rho\!=\!0$) causes model collapse (HM=0.233). Without constraint enforcement, the forget objective dominates and destroys retain performance. This confirms that adaptive constraint enforcement is essential, not optional.

\paragraph{LoRA parameterization (A15).} Full fine-tuning without LoRA (A15) gives HM=0.459 with catastrophic retain drift (ret=0.306). The low-rank bottleneck is critical for preventing the unlearning update from affecting weights far beyond those encoding the forget knowledge.

\paragraph{Clamped entropy vs.\ alternatives (A4, A5, A17).} Replacing clamped entropy with gradient ascent (A4: HM=0.160), NPO (A5: HM=0.009), or logit margin (A17: HM=0.000) causes catastrophic failure even with the full bilevel ALM apparatus intact. GA causes retain collapse through unbounded gradients; NPO's objective is trivially satisfied without actual forgetting because its reference-based formulation does not enforce distributional uncertainty; logit margin produces unbounded gradients that overwhelm the inner loop's repair capacity.

\paragraph{Bilevel structure (A1, A18).} Removing the inner loop ($K\!=\!0$, A1) causes a modest retain drop (0.658$\to$0.631, HM 0.823$\to$0.819). The ALM constraint alone provides substantial protection, but the inner loop adds a safety margin. Swapping the bilevel structure (A18, forgetting in inner loop) causes HM to drop to 0.506 as the dual variable $\lambda$ explodes without convergence---confirming the ``repair first, forget carefully'' principle.

\paragraph{Entropy clamping threshold (A9).} Setting $\tau\!=\!1.0$ (unclamped, A9) drops HM from 0.823 to 0.780, with retain falling from 0.658 to 0.604. Without clamping, the objective applies gratuitous gradient pressure to already-forgotten tokens, destabilizing shared representations.

\paragraph{Three-phase convergence.}
\figref{fig:dynamics} shows that \method follows the same dynamics across all settings (models from 1B to 7B, datasets from 20 QA pairs to full books):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Warm-up} (steps 0--$T_{\text{trans}}$): $\Loss_{\text{forget}}$ declines gradually while $L_{\text{ret}}$ stays near $\varepsilon$. The forget objective makes progress without disturbing retain.
    \item \textbf{Spike and ratchet} ($T_{\text{trans}}$--$T_{\text{trans}}$+30): accumulated forgetting disrupts shared representations, causing a retain spike. The quadratic penalty activates and $\lambda$ jumps from ${\sim}1.0$ to ${\sim}2.2$--$2.6$, permanently increasing retain protection.
    \item \textbf{Smooth convergence}: $\Loss_{\text{forget}}$ continues toward zero under the elevated $\lambda$, with no further retain violations. The clamped entropy loss self-stabilizes as forgotten tokens drop out.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Comparison with PDU.}
PDU \citep{entesari2025constrained} also uses a primal-dual formulation, but with two critical differences visible in the dynamics: (i)~its forget loss (logit margin) is unbounded, starting at 700+ and never reaching zero; and (ii)~its retain loss oscillates repeatedly throughout training. On MUSE Books (\figref{fig:dynamics_pdu_books}), PDU's retain loss spikes at steps 110, 125, 135, 150, and 170---each spike represents uncontrolled retain degradation. On TOFU (\figref{fig:dynamics_pdu_tofu}), PDU's retain loss grows monotonically from 0.14 to 0.34 across just 10 steps.

In contrast, \method's asymmetric ratchet resolves the retain constraint in a \emph{single} episode: after the first spike, $\lambda$ stays elevated and no subsequent violations occur. This one-shot calibration explains why \method achieves near-zero forget metrics while maintaining retain performance.

\paragraph{Scaling behavior.}
On harder settings (larger forget sets), $\lambda$ rises higher---from ${\sim}2.4$ on forget01 to ${\sim}4.5$ on forget10---reflecting that more retain protection is needed when more data is being forgotten. This adaptation happens automatically via the dual update, without any hyperparameter change.
