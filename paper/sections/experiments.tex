\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmarks}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

We evaluate on two complementary benchmarks:

\paragraph{TOFU} \citep{maini2024tofu} contains 200 fictitious author profiles with 20 QA pairs each (4000 total). We use the forget01 split (1\% of authors, 20 QA pairs). Metrics: Model Utility (MU, harmonic mean of 9 sub-metrics on retain/real-authors/world-facts), Forget Quality (FQ, KS-test $p$-value on truth ratio distributions), Extraction Strength (ES), forget answer probability (fgt\_Prob), and forget ROUGE-L (fgt\_ROUGE). We report HM = hmean(MU, 1$-$fgt\_Prob, 1$-$fgt\_ROUGE) as the overall score.

\paragraph{MUSE Books} \citep{shi2024muse} targets removal of Harry Potter book content from Llama-2-7B. Metrics: forget\_knowmem (knowledge memorization), verbmem (verbatim memorization), retain\_knowmem (retain knowledge), extraction strength. We report HM = hmean(1$-$forget\_knowmem, 1$-$verbmem, retain\_knowmem).

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We compare against eight baselines, all using their published default hyperparameters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{GradAscent} \citep{jang2023knowledge}: maximize CE on forget data.
    \item \textbf{GradDiff} \citep{yao2023large}: weighted sum of forget GA and retain CE.
    \item \textbf{NPO} \citep{zhang2024negative}: DPO-style preference optimization against memorized outputs.
    \item \textbf{SimNPO} \citep{fan2024simplicity}: reference-free variant of NPO.
    \item \textbf{BLURNPO} \citep{reisizadeh2025blur}: bilevel formulation with gradient projection.
    \item \textbf{RMU} \citep{li2024wmdp}: representation misdirection toward random targets.
    \item \textbf{PDU} \citep{entesari2025constrained}: primal-dual constrained optimization with logit-margin loss.
    \item \textbf{Gold (retrain)}: model retrained from scratch without forget data (upper bound on retain, lower bound on memorization).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Implementation Details}
\label{sec:implementation}

\paragraph{MUSE Books (Llama-2-7B).}
LoRA $r\!=\!16$, $\alpha\!=\!32$ on all projections (q/k/v/o, gate/up/down).
$K\!=\!3$, $\eta_{\text{in}}\!=\!2\text{e-}4$, $\eta_{\text{out}}\!=\!3\text{e-}5$, $\varepsilon\!=\!0.15$, $\rho\!=\!0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{init}}\!=\!1.0$, $\tau\!=\!0.7$.
Batch size 2, gradient accumulation 8 (effective batch size 16). 4 epochs.

\paragraph{TOFU (Llama-3.2-1B/3B).}
LoRA $r\!=\!8$, $\alpha\!=\!16$.
$K\!=\!3$, $\eta_{\text{in}}\!=\!2\text{e-}4$, $\eta_{\text{out}}\!=\!2\text{e-}5$, $\varepsilon_{\text{mul}}\!=\!0.85$, $\rho\!=\!0.1$, $\lambda_{\text{init}}\!=\!1.0$, $\tau\!=\!0.7$.
Batch size 4, gradient accumulation 4 (effective batch size 16). $T\!=\!100$ and $T\!=\!150$ steps.

All experiments run on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU.

Tables~\ref{tab:tofu_1b} and~\ref{tab:tofu_3b} show TOFU results. On 1B, \method at $T$=150 achieves HM=0.811 vs.\ PDU (0.806). At $T$=100, \method achieves the highest Forget Quality (FQ=0.919) among all methods---meaning the forget set's truth ratio distribution is nearly indistinguishable from the gold retrained model's. On 3B, \method is slightly below PDU (0.852 vs.\ 0.855) while achieving near-perfect forgetting (fgt\_Prob$<$0.001, fgt\_ROUGE=0.012).

\paragraph{Note on margins.} The TOFU improvements are small (0.005 on 1B, $-$0.003 on 3B) and based on single runs. We report these as competitive rather than claiming clear superiority. The stronger signal is on MUSE Books ($+$0.043 over SimNPO) and on forgetting quality metrics (FQ, fgt\_Prob) where \method consistently achieves near-perfect scores.

\paragraph{Note on ``beating gold.''}
On MUSE Books, \method achieves higher HM than the gold retrained model (0.798 vs.\ 0.739). This is because the gold model retains residual knowledge of the forget content (fk=0.303) since general pretraining corpora overlap with the Harry Potter books. \method achieves lower forget knowledge (fk=0.080) at the cost of lower retain (0.598 vs.\ 0.687). This represents a different operating point on the Pareto frontier, not a strictly superior solution---the gold model defines correct behavior, and over-forgetting relative to it may indicate collateral damage to related knowledge.

\figref{fig:dynamics} shows loss dynamics for all three settings. Despite different model scales (1B, 3B, 7B) and datasets (TOFU QA pairs vs.\ MUSE book text), all runs exhibit the same three-phase pattern:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Warm-up:} $\Loss_{\text{forget}}$ declines gradually while $L_{\text{ret}}$ stays near $\varepsilon$. $\lambda$ drifts slowly.
    \item \textbf{Spike and ratchet:} $L_{\text{ret}}$ spikes as accumulated forgetting disrupts shared representations. The quadratic penalty activates and $\lambda$ ratchets from ${\sim}1.0$ to ${\sim}2.2$--$2.4$. $L_{\text{ret}}$ recovers within 6--30 steps.
    \item \textbf{Smooth convergence:} Elevated $\lambda$ permanently increases retain protection. $\Loss_{\text{forget}}$ continues to near-zero. No further spikes occur.
\end{enumerate}

This consistency across settings suggests the ALM mechanism is discovering a universal forget--retain tradeoff point, with final $\lambda$ values converging to ${\sim}2.2$--$2.4$ regardless of model size or dataset.
