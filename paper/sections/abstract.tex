\begin{abstract}

Large language models memorize training data, creating legal, privacy, and safety risks that demand targeted knowledge removal without full retraining.
Current unlearning methods pair unbounded forget objectives---which destroy model coherence---with fixed-weight loss balancing that cannot track the shifting difficulty of retain preservation during training.

We propose \method, a bilevel optimization framework for LLM unlearning.
The forget objective is \emph{clamped entropy}: per-token output entropy is maximized only up to a fraction $\tau$ of maximum entropy $H_{\max} = \log V$, after which the gradient is exactly zero.
This gives a bounded loss whose active set shrinks as tokens are forgotten, naturally decelerating optimization.
Retain preservation is enforced as an inequality constraint $\Loss_\text{ret} \leq \varepsilon$ via an augmented Lagrangian with an asymmetric dual update---violations ratchet $\lambda$ up fast, satisfaction lets it decay slowly---nested inside a bilevel structure where the inner loop runs retain repair and the outer loop drives forgetting.
All updates are confined to LoRA adapters.

Experiments cover TOFU (Llama-3.2-1B/3B, three forget splits, 5 seeds), MUSE Books and News (Llama-2-7B), and KnowUndo (Llama-2-7B-chat, copyright/privacy).
\method achieves HM=0.808 on TOFU 1B forget01 versus 0.690 for the strongest baseline, wins all 6 TOFU settings under an LLM judge, and reaches HM=0.823 on MUSE Books---surpassing even the gold retrained model.
An ablation confirms that removing any single component causes failure, and dynamics analysis shows a consistent three-phase convergence pattern absent from baselines.

\end{abstract}
