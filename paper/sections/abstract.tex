\begin{abstract}

Large language models memorize training data, which creates legal, privacy, and safety risks that call for targeted knowledge removal without degrading general capabilities.
Existing unlearning methods use unbounded forget objectives that destroy model coherence, or lack mechanisms to protect retain quality during aggressive forgetting.

We propose \method, a bilevel optimization framework that treats retain preservation as a hard constraint via an augmented Lagrangian.
The outer loop minimizes a \emph{clamped entropy} forget loss that vanishes once per-token entropy exceeds a threshold, giving bounded and self-stabilizing forgetting.
An asymmetric dual update permanently calibrates the forget--retain tradeoff after the first constraint violation.
All updates are confined to LoRA adapters.

We evaluate on TOFU (Llama-3.2-1B/3B, three forget splits, 5 seeds) and MUSE Books (Llama-2-7B).
On TOFU 1B, \method achieves HM=0.808 vs.\ the strongest baseline PDU at 0.690 ($+$0.118).
The gap holds across splits and scales, with \method winning all 6 LLM judge settings.
On MUSE Books, \method reaches HM=0.798, surpassing SimNPO (0.755) by $+$0.043.

\end{abstract}
