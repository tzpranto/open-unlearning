\begin{abstract}

Large language models memorize training data, creating legal, privacy, and safety risks that require targeted removal of specific knowledge without degrading general capabilities.
Existing unlearning methods use unbounded forget objectives (gradient ascent, NPO) that destroy model coherence, or lack principled mechanisms to enforce retain quality during aggressive forgetting.

We propose \method, a bilevel optimization framework for LLM unlearning that treats retain preservation as a hard constraint rather than a soft penalty.
The inner loop runs $K$ SGD steps on retain data to maintain utility; the outer loop minimizes a \emph{clamped entropy} forget loss subject to an augmented Lagrangian constraint on retain performance.
Clamped entropy maximizes per-token output entropy only up to a threshold $\tau \cdot H_{\max}$, after which the gradient vanishes---providing bounded, self-stabilizing forgetting where tokens ``drop out'' as they are forgotten.
An asymmetric dual update ratchets the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ quickly on constraint violations but decays it slowly when satisfied, permanently calibrating the forget--retain tradeoff after the first retain spike.
All updates are confined to LoRA adapters, which limit the damage radius and provide a free reference model by disabling adapters.

On MUSE Books (Llama-2-7B), \method achieves HM=0.798, surpassing the best baseline SimNPO (0.755) and the gold retrained model (0.739).
On TOFU forget01 (Llama-3.2-1B), \method achieves HM=0.811, outperforming PDU (0.806), with the highest forget quality (FQ=0.919) among all methods at $T$=100 steps.
On TOFU 3B, \method matches PDU (HM=0.852 vs 0.855) while achieving near-perfect forgetting (fgt\_Prob=0.000).
Loss dynamics across all settings exhibit the same three-phase pattern: warm-up, spike-and-ratchet, and smooth convergence---demonstrating that the ALM mechanism generalizes across model scales and datasets.

\end{abstract}
