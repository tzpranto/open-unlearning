\begin{abstract}

Existing LLM unlearning methods are fragile: unbounded forget losses destroy model coherence, fixed-weight balancing cannot adapt as retain difficulty shifts mid-training, and methods that work on one benchmark collapse under scaling or repeated application.
We propose \method, a constrained bilevel framework that produces robust, smooth, and manageable unlearning.
The forget objective is \emph{clamped entropy}---per-token entropy maximized only to a vocabulary-invariant threshold $\tau \cdot \log V$, after which the gradient is exactly zero---yielding a bounded, self-decelerating loss that requires no reference model.
Retain quality is enforced as a hard inequality constraint via an augmented Lagrangian with asymmetric dual updates that permanently ratchet protection after any violation, nested in a bilevel structure where the inner loop repairs retain damage before each outer forgetting step.
All updates are confined to LoRA adapters, bounding representational drift.
Across TOFU (1B/3B, three splits), MUSE (Books/News), and KnowUndo (copyright/privacy), \method achieves the highest composite scores in 6 of 7 settings under both standard metrics and an LLM judge, and degrades gracefully under $4\times$ scaling and 4 sequential unlearning steps where the strongest baseline collapses entirely.
Training consistently follows a three-phase pattern---warm-up, spike-and-ratchet, smooth convergence---providing interpretable, predictable optimization absent from all baselines.

\end{abstract}
