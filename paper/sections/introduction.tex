\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models memorize substantial portions of their training data \citep{carlini2021extracting}, raising concerns around copyright, privacy, and retention of hazardous knowledge.
\emph{Machine unlearning} removes specific knowledge from a trained model without the cost of retraining from scratch---prohibitive for billion-parameter models trained on trillions of tokens.

The core difficulty is the \emph{forget--retain tradeoff}. Aggressive removal of memorized content perturbs representations shared with benign knowledge, degrading the model's general capabilities. Existing methods handle this through loss balancing: a forget term plus a retain term with fixed weights \citep{liu2025rethinking, zhang2024negative}. Fixed coefficients cannot adapt as training progresses and the difficulty of retaining shifts.

Three failure modes recur in current approaches:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Unbounded forgetting.} Gradient ascent \citep{jang2023knowledge} and logit-margin losses produce arbitrarily large gradients on highly memorized tokens, causing utility collapse even with retain regularization.
    \item \textbf{Static tradeoff.} Fixed-weight combinations (GradDiff, NPO) cannot react when retain performance degrades mid-training.
    \item \textbf{Over-forgetting.} Unclamped entropy maximization \citep{yuan2025closer} and gradient ascent continue to push already-forgotten tokens, wasting gradient budget and destabilizing the model.
\end{enumerate}

\method addresses all three through two contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Clamped entropy loss} (\secref{sec:clamped_entropy}): per-token output entropy is maximized up to a vocabulary-invariant threshold $\tau \cdot H_{\max}$, where $H_{\max} = \log V$. Once a token's entropy crosses this threshold, its gradient contribution is exactly zero---it has been forgotten and drops out of optimization. The loss is bounded in $[0, \tau \cdot H_{\max}]$ and shrinks monotonically as training succeeds. No reference model is needed.
    \item \textbf{Inequality-constrained bilevel formulation} (\secref{sec:bilevel}, \secref{sec:alm}): the inner loop runs fast SGD on the retain set, repairing damage before each outer step drives forgetting. Retain performance is enforced as a hard constraint $\Loss_\text{ret} \leq \varepsilon$ via an augmented Lagrangian whose asymmetric dual update ratchets protection up on violation but decays slowly when satisfied. This ``repair first, forget carefully'' design inverts prior bilevel methods.
    \item \textbf{LoRA parameterization} (\secref{sec:lora}): all updates are confined to low-rank adapters, limiting how far the unlearning update can push the model from its pretrained state.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate on four benchmarks: TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu} with Llama-3.2-1B and 3B across three forget splits (5 seeds each), MUSE Books and News \citep{shi2025muse} with Llama-2-7B, and KnowUndo \citep{tian2024knowundo} with Llama-2-7B-chat on copyright and privacy domains.
On TOFU 1B forget01, \method achieves HM=0.808 versus the strongest baseline PDU at 0.690 ($+$0.118).
\method wins 4 of 6 TOFU settings on automated metrics and all 6 under an LLM judge, which reveals near-zero forget leakage (FL=0.015/2.0) where the best baseline still leaks substantially (FL=0.855).
On MUSE Books, \method reaches HM=0.823, surpassing both the retrained gold model (0.739) and the best baseline (0.753).
On KnowUndo, it achieves the best harmonic mean on both copyright (0.482) and privacy (0.602).
On MUSE News---a harder setting with 889 independent articles---\method is competitive (0.542) but PDU leads (0.577), pointing to a limitation of global $\lambda$ on heterogeneous forget sets.

Beyond end-point metrics, we provide a full ablation and dynamics analysis. Training consistently follows a three-phase pattern: warm-up, spike-and-ratchet, smooth convergence. Baselines show recurring retain oscillations with no self-correction. The ablation confirms that each component is necessary: removing any one causes catastrophic failure or significant degradation.
