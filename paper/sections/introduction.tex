\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) memorize substantial portions of their training data \citep{carlini2021extracting}. This creates risks including copyright infringement, privacy violations, and retention of hazardous knowledge.
\emph{Machine unlearning} aims to remove specific knowledge from a trained model without full retraining, which is computationally prohibitive for models with billions of parameters trained on trillions of tokens.

The central challenge in LLM unlearning is the \emph{forget--retain tradeoff}: aggressively removing memorized content perturbs shared representations and degrades the model's general capabilities.
Existing methods handle this tradeoff through loss balancing, combining a forget loss with a retain loss using fixed weights \citep{liu2025rethinking, zhang2024negative}. But fixed coefficients cannot adapt to the changing dynamics of unlearning, since the difficulty of retain preservation shifts as forgetting progresses.

We identify three failure modes in current approaches:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Unbounded forgetting.} Gradient ascent \citep{jang2023knowledge} and logit-based objectives produce arbitrarily large gradients on highly memorized tokens. This causes catastrophic utility loss even with retain regularization.
    \item \textbf{Static tradeoff.} Fixed-weight combinations of forget and retain losses (GradDiff, NPO) cannot adapt when retain performance suddenly degrades during training.
    \item \textbf{Over-forgetting.} Unclamped entropy maximization \citep{yuan2025closer} and gradient ascent keep pushing already-forgotten tokens. This wastes gradient budget and destabilizes the model.
\end{enumerate}

We address these with \method, a bilevel optimization framework whose two main contributions are a novel forget objective and a novel bilevel constrained formulation:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Clamped entropy loss} (\secref{sec:clamped_entropy}): we maximize per-token output entropy up to a vocabulary-invariant threshold $\tau \cdot H_{\max}$, where $H_{\max} = \log V$. The threshold $\tau$ has a direct information-theoretic interpretation: a token is ``forgotten'' when its distribution reaches $\tau$ fraction of maximum uncertainty, regardless of vocabulary size. The $\max(0, \cdot)$ clamp creates a hard deadzone---once forgotten, a token contributes exactly zero gradient, bounding the loss in $[0, \tau \cdot H_{\max}]$ and making optimization self-stabilizing without any scheduling. No prior unlearning method provides this per-token stopping criterion.
    \item \textbf{Inequality-constrained bilevel formulation} (\secref{sec:bilevel}, \secref{sec:alm}): we separate the retain and forget objectives into inner and outer loops of a bilevel optimization---the inner loop rapidly repairs retain performance via $K$ SGD steps, while the outer loop drives forgetting subject to a hard inequality constraint $\Loss_{\text{ret}} \leq \varepsilon$. The constraint is enforced via an augmented Lagrangian with an asymmetric dual update that ratchets up protection on violations but decays slowly when satisfied. This ``repair first, forget carefully'' structure is the inverse of prior bilevel unlearning methods that put forgetting in the inner loop.
    \item \textbf{LoRA parameterization} (\secref{sec:lora}): we confine all updates to low-rank adapters, which limits how much the unlearning update can affect other weights.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate \method on four benchmarks spanning two model families (Llama-2, Llama-3.2) and three scales (1B, 3B, 7B):
TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu} across two model scales and three forget splits (5 seeds each),
MUSE Books and News \citep{shi2025muse} with Llama-2-7B,
and KnowUndo \citep{tian2024knowundo} with Llama-2-7B-chat on copyright and privacy domains.
On TOFU 1B forget01, \method achieves HM=0.808 versus the strongest baseline PDU (0.690), an improvement of $+$0.118.
\method wins 4 of 6 TOFU settings on automated HM and all 6 on an LLM judge, with the largest gains on small forget sets where the forget--retain boundary is tightest.
The LLM judge reveals near-zero forget leakage for \method (FL=0.015/2.0 on forget01) while the strongest baseline still leaks substantially (FL=0.855).
On MUSE Books, \method achieves HM=0.823, the highest among all tested methods.
On KnowUndo, \method achieves the best harmonic mean on both copyright (0.479 vs.\ PDU 0.456) and privacy (0.601 vs.\ PDU 0.552) domains, with the LLM judge confirming the highest retain accuracy.
On MUSE News---a harder distributed multi-document setting---\method is competitive (HM=0.542) though PDU leads (0.581), highlighting the challenge of heterogeneous forget sets.

Beyond end-point metrics, we provide a complete ablation study and dynamics-level analysis showing that \method's training consistently follows a three-phase pattern---warm-up, spike-and-ratchet, smooth convergence---while baselines exhibit recurring retain oscillations with no self-correcting mechanism. The ablation confirms that each component (clamped entropy, bilevel structure, ALM constraint, LoRA parameterization) is necessary: removing any one causes catastrophic failure or significant degradation.
