\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Large language models (LLMs) memorize substantial portions of their training data \citep{carlini2021extracting}, creating risks ranging from copyright infringement to privacy violations to retention of hazardous knowledge.
\emph{Machine unlearning} aims to remove specific knowledge from a trained model without full retraining---a computationally prohibitive alternative for models with billions of parameters trained on trillions of tokens.

The central challenge in LLM unlearning is the \emph{forget--retain tradeoff}: aggressively removing memorized content inevitably perturbs shared representations, degrading the model's general capabilities.
Existing methods address this tradeoff through loss balancing---combining a forget loss with a retain loss using fixed weights \citep{liu2022continual, zhang2024negative}---but fixed coefficients cannot adapt to the non-stationary dynamics of unlearning, where the difficulty of retain preservation changes as forgetting progresses.

We identify three failure modes in current approaches:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Unbounded forgetting.} Gradient ascent \citep{jang2023knowledge} and logit-based objectives produce arbitrarily large gradients on highly memorized tokens, causing catastrophic utility loss even with retain regularization.
    \item \textbf{Static tradeoff.} Fixed-weight combinations of forget and retain losses (GradDiff, NPO) cannot adapt when retain performance suddenly degrades mid-training.
    \item \textbf{Over-forgetting.} Unclamped entropy maximization \citep{yuan2024closer} and gradient ascent continue pushing already-forgotten tokens, wasting gradient budget and destabilizing the model.
\end{enumerate}

\method addresses all three through a bilevel optimization framework with four interlocking components:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Clamped entropy loss} (\secref{sec:clamped_entropy}): maximizes per-token output entropy up to a threshold $\tau \cdot H_{\max}$, after which the gradient vanishes. This provides bounded, self-stabilizing forgetting---tokens ``drop out'' of the loss as they are forgotten.
    \item \textbf{Augmented Lagrangian constraint} (\secref{sec:alm}): treats retain preservation as a hard constraint $\Loss_{\text{ret}} \leq \varepsilon$ enforced by a learned Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$, rather than a soft penalty with a fixed weight.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric dual update} (\secref{sec:alm}): ratchets $\lambda$ quickly on constraint violations but decays it slowly when satisfied, permanently calibrating the tradeoff after the first retain spike.
    \item \textbf{LoRA parameterization} (\secref{sec:lora}): confines all updates to low-rank adapters, limiting damage radius and providing a free reference model by simply disabling adapters.
\end{enumerate}

We evaluate \method on MUSE Books \citep{shi2024muse} and TOFU \citep{maini2024tofu} across three model scales (1B, 3B, 7B).
On MUSE Books, \method achieves HM=0.798, surpassing SimNPO (0.755) and the gold retrained model (0.739).
On TOFU 1B, \method achieves HM=0.811, outperforming PDU (0.806).
Loss dynamics across all settings exhibit the same three-phase pattern---warm-up, spike-and-ratchet, smooth convergence---demonstrating that the ALM mechanism generalizes across scales and datasets.
