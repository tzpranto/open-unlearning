\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{app:theory}

We provide formal justification for three key design choices in \method:
(i)~bounded loss and gradient control of the clamped entropy objective (\S\ref{app:bounded_grad}),
(ii)~the ratchet convergence behavior of the asymmetric dual variable update (\S\ref{app:ratchet}), and
(iii)~the selective uncertainty property enabled by sub-maximal entropy clamping (\S\ref{app:selective}).
Together, these results explain why \method avoids the catastrophic model collapse commonly observed with unbounded unlearning objectives.


\subsection{Bounded Loss and Gradient of Clamped Entropy}
\label{app:bounded_grad}

Let $V$ denote the vocabulary size, $\tau \in (0,1)$ a clamping ratio, and $p_{\thetav}(\cdot \mid x_{<t})$ the next-token distribution produced by a language model with parameters $\thetav$.
Define the per-token Shannon entropy $H_t(\thetav) = -\sum_{v=1}^{V} p_{\thetav}(v \mid x_{<t}) \log p_{\thetav}(v \mid x_{<t})$ and the clamped entropy loss for a single token as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:clamp_token}
\ell_t(\thetav) \;=\; \max\!\bigl(0,\;\tau \log V - H_t(\thetav)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For a sequence of $T$ tokens the forget loss is $\Loss_{\mathrm{fgt}}(\thetav) = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \ell_t(\thetav)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 1} (Bounded Loss and Gradient of Clamped Entropy).
\textit{Assume:
(A1)~the logit map $z_t : \R^d \to \R^V$ is $G$-Lipschitz, i.e., $\lVert \nabla_{\thetav} z_t(\thetav) \rVert_F \leq G$; and
(A2)~the logit magnitudes are bounded, $\lVert z_t(\thetav) \rVert_\infty \leq B$, for all tokens $t$ and parameters $\thetav$.
Then:}

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textit{The loss is bounded: $0 \leq \ell_t(\thetav) \leq \tau \log V$ for all $\thetav$.}
\item[(b)] \textit{The per-token gradient norm satisfies}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:grad_bound}
\bigl\lVert \nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t(\thetav) \bigr\rVert \;\leq\; (2B + 2\log V)\, G.
\end{equation}
\item[(c)] \textit{In contrast, the gradient ascent loss $\Loss_\mathrm{GA} = -\log p_{\thetav}(y_t \mid x_{<t})$ has unbounded loss values as $p_{\thetav}(y_t) \to 0$, and unclamped entropy maximization $\Loss_\mathrm{ent} = -H_t$ applies nonzero gradients even when $H_t$ is already near $H_\mathrm{max}$.}
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textit{Proof.}
(a)~Since $0 \leq H_t \leq \log V$ and $\tau \in (0,1)$, the ReLU outputs a value in $[0, \tau \log V]$.

(b)~In the active region ($H_t < \tau \log V$), the chain rule gives $\nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t = -\nabla_{\thetav} H_t$.
We derive a component-wise bound on $\nabla_{z_t} H_t$ that avoids the $\sqrt{V}$ factor introduced by spectral-norm arguments.
The $v$-th component of the entropy gradient with respect to logits is:
\[
\frac{\partial H_t}{\partial z_{t,v}} = -p_v\bigl(H_t + \log p_v\bigr),
\]
which follows from the softmax Jacobian identity $\partial p_j / \partial z_{t,v} = p_j(\delta_{jv} - p_v)$ applied to $H_t = -\sum_v p_v \log p_v$.
One can verify correctness at the uniform distribution: $p_v = 1/V$ gives $H_t = \log V$ and $\log p_v = -\log V$, so $H_t + \log p_v = 0$ and the gradient vanishes, as expected at the entropy maximum.
Under assumption~(A2), the softmax probabilities satisfy $p_v \geq e^{-2B}/V$ for all $v$, since
$p_v = e^{z_v}/\sum_j e^{z_j} \geq e^{-B}/(V e^{B}) = e^{-2B}/V$.
This yields $|\!\log p_v| \leq 2B + \log V$ and $H_t \leq \log V$, so $|H_t + \log p_v| \leq H_t + |\!\log p_v| \leq 2B + 2\log V$.
Since $p_v^2 \leq p_v$ for $p_v \in [0,1]$:
\begin{multline*}
\lVert \nabla_{z_t} H_t \rVert^2 = \sum_v p_v^2 (H_t + \log p_v)^2 \\
\leq (2B + 2\log V)^2 \sum_v p_v = (2B + 2\log V)^2.
\end{multline*}
By the chain rule and assumption~(A1):
\begin{multline*}
\lVert \nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t \rVert = \lVert \nabla_{\thetav} H_t \rVert \leq \lVert \nabla_{z_t} H_t \rVert \cdot \lVert \nabla_{\thetav} z_t \rVert_F \\
\leq (2B + 2\log V) \cdot G.
\end{multline*}
In the inactive region ($H_t \geq \tau \log V$), $\nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t = \mathbf{0}$.
Averaging over $T$ tokens preserves the bound.

(c)~For GA, $-\!\log p_{\thetav}(y_t) \to \infty$ as $p_{\thetav}(y_t) \to 0$, so the loss is unbounded.
For unclamped entropy maximization, $\nabla_{\thetav}(-H_t) \neq \mathbf{0}$ whenever the distribution is non-uniform, so the objective exerts gradient force even when $H_t$ is close to $H_\mathrm{max}$ and forgetting is essentially complete.
By contrast, clamped entropy produces \emph{exactly zero} gradient once $H_t \geq \tau \log V$, eliminating wasted optimization pressure.
\hfill$\square$

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark.}
Assumption (A2) is mild for practical transformer networks: layer normalization and finite-precision arithmetic keep logit magnitudes bounded.
For Llama-2-7b with $V = 32000$ and typical logit ranges $B \approx 15$--$30$, the bound in \eqref{eq:grad_bound} evaluates to approximately $(2 \cdot 30 + 2 \cdot 10.4) \cdot G \approx 81\,G$, a moderate constant.
In contrast, the GA gradient $\lVert \nabla_{\thetav}(-\!\log p_{y_t}) \rVert$ has no such guarantee as training drives $p_{y_t} \to 0$ on forget tokens.


\subsection{Dual Variable Ratchet Convergence}
\label{app:ratchet}

\method enforces the retain-quality constraint $\Loss_\mathrm{ret}(\thetav) \leq \varepsilon$ via an augmented Lagrangian with asymmetric dual updates.
Let $r_t = \Loss_\mathrm{ret}(\thetav_t) - \varepsilon$ denote the constraint residual at iteration $t$, $\rho > 0$ the penalty coefficient, $\alpha \in (0,1)$ the asymmetry ratio (we use $\alpha = 0.1$), and $\lambda_\mathrm{min} > 0$ a dual variable floor.
The dual variable is updated as:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dual_update_app}
\lambda_{t+1} = \max\!\Bigl(\lambda_\mathrm{min},\; \lambda_t + \begin{cases} \rho \, r_t & \text{if } r_t > 0, \\ \alpha \, \rho \, r_t & \text{if } r_t \leq 0. \end{cases}\Bigr)
\end{equation}

We model training as proceeding through \emph{episodes}, a simplifying abstraction motivated by the empirical observation that constraint violations cluster around epoch boundaries (see the remark below).
Each episode $k$ consists of a violation phase ($r_t > 0$ for $n_k^+$ consecutive steps with mean violation $\bar{r}_k^+ > 0$) followed by a recovery phase ($r_t \leq 0$ for $n_k^-$ steps with mean slack $\bar{r}_k^- < 0$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 2} (Dual Variable Ratchet).
\textit{Assume:
(B1)~the dual floor satisfies $\lambda_\mathrm{min} > 0$ (so $\lambda_t \geq \lambda_\mathrm{min}$ at all times);
(B2)~the asymmetry condition $n_k^+ \bar{r}_k^+ > \alpha \, n_k^- |\bar{r}_k^-|$ holds for each episode $k$; and
(B3)~there exists a dual threshold $\lambda^*$ such that the ALM objective $\Loss_\mathrm{fgt} + \lambda(\Loss_\mathrm{ret} - \varepsilon) + \frac{\rho}{2}\max(0,r)^2$ admits a feasible stationary point with $r \leq 0$ whenever $\lambda \geq \lambda^*$.
Then:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textit{The net dual change per episode satisfies $\Delta\lambda_k \geq \rho(n_k^+ \bar{r}_k^+ - \alpha\, n_k^- |\bar{r}_k^-|) > 0$.}
\item[(b)] \textit{After at most $K^* = \lceil (\lambda^* - \lambda_0) / (\rho \, \underline{\Delta}) \rceil$ episodes, where $\underline{\Delta} = \min_k (n_k^+ \bar{r}_k^+ - \alpha\, n_k^- |\bar{r}_k^-|)$, the dual variable satisfies $\lambda_t \geq \lambda^*$ and no further violation episodes occur.}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.}
(a) Since $\lambda_\mathrm{min} > 0$ and the violation phase increments $\lambda$ by $\rho r_t > 0$ at each step, $\lambda_t$ remains strictly above $\lambda_\mathrm{min}$ throughout the violation phase.
During recovery, each decrement is $\alpha\rho|r_t|$, which is $\alpha$ times as large as the corresponding increment would be.
The $\max(\lambda_\mathrm{min}, \cdot)$ projection can only \emph{increase} $\lambda_t$ relative to the unprojected update (it clips negative overshoots), so the cumulative change satisfies:
\begin{align*}
\Delta\lambda_k &= \lambda_{t_{k+1}} - \lambda_{t_k} \\
&\geq \rho \sum_{t \in \mathrm{viol}} r_t + \alpha\rho \sum_{t \in \mathrm{recov}} r_t \\
&= \rho\bigl(n_k^+ \bar{r}_k^+ - \alpha\, n_k^- |\bar{r}_k^-|\bigr) > 0,
\end{align*}
where the inequality uses the fact that $\max(\lambda_\mathrm{min}, x) \geq x$ for all $x$, so projecting can only reduce the total decrease.
The final inequality follows from assumption~(B2).

The asymmetry ratio $\alpha = 0.1$ makes (B2) easy to satisfy: even if the recovery phase is $10\times$ longer than the violation phase with equal magnitude, the condition holds with equality.
In practice, violation spikes are sharp (large $\bar{r}_k^+$, small $n_k^+$) while recovery is gradual, so the condition is satisfied with margin.

(b) Since $\Delta\lambda_k \geq \rho\,\underline{\Delta} > 0$ for each episode, after $K$ episodes:
\[
\lambda_{t_K} \;\geq\; \lambda_0 + K\rho\,\underline{\Delta}.
\]
Setting $K = K^*$ gives $\lambda_{t_{K^*}} \geq \lambda^*$.
By assumption~(B3), for $\lambda_t \geq \lambda^*$ the outer-loop optimizer can reach a point with $r_t \leq 0$, so no further violation phase begins.
In the absence of violations, $\lambda_t$ decreases slowly at rate $\alpha\rho|r_t|$ per step toward $\lambda^*$ from above.
\hfill$\square$

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Remark on assumptions.}
Assumption~(B1) corresponds to the hyperparameter $\lambda_\mathrm{min} = 0.1$ in our implementation, which prevents the dual variable from vanishing.
Assumption~(B3) is standard in ALM convergence theory \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear}: it requires that the constrained problem is feasible for the given parameterization --- i.e., there exist LoRA parameters achieving $\Loss_\mathrm{ret} \leq \varepsilon$.
The episode structure in~(B2) is a modeling simplification; in practice, violations may be interleaved with recoveries, but the key property --- that asymmetric updates cause net $\lambda$ increase whenever total violation exceeds $\alpha$ times total slack --- holds regardless of ordering.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Empirical validation.}
In our MUSE Books experiments, $\lambda$ is initialized at $1.0$ and spikes to $\approx 2.27$ after a single violation episode at the epoch~1--2 boundary, then stabilizes.
One episode with $\Delta\lambda_1 \approx 1.27$ suffices to cross $\lambda^*$, after which no further violations are observed across three subsequent epochs.
A symmetric update ($\alpha = 1$) would decrease $\lambda$ equally fast during recovery, risking oscillatory violations.


\subsection{Selective Uncertainty via Entropy Clamping}
\label{app:selective}

We formalize the intuition that sub-maximal entropy clamping ($\tau < 1$) enables the model to be uncertain on forget data while remaining confident on retain data.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proposition 3} (Selective Uncertainty).
\textit{Let $H_{\max} = \log V$ denote the maximum per-token entropy.
Consider a model $p_{\thetav}$ trained with the clamped entropy objective \eqref{eq:clamp_token} with threshold $\tau \in (0,1)$.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] \textit{(Zero gradient above threshold.)
For every forget token $t$ with $H_t(\thetav) \geq \tau H_{\max}$, the gradient contribution $\nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t = \mathbf{0}$.}
\item[(b)] \textit{(Gradient force at logit level for unclamped entropy.)
For unclamped entropy maximization, $\Loss_\mathrm{ent} = -\frac{1}{T}\sum_t H_t$, the logit-level gradient satisfies $\nabla_{z_t} H_t = \mathbf{0}$ if and only if $p_{\thetav}(\cdot \mid x_{<t})$ is uniform over $V$.
Hence $\Loss_\mathrm{ent}$ exerts a nonzero logit-level gradient on every token whose output distribution is non-uniform.}
\item[(c)] \textit{(Decoupling at convergence.)
At a stationary point of the combined objective $\Loss_\mathrm{fgt} + \lambda \Loss_\mathrm{ret}$ where all forget tokens satisfy $H_t > \tau H_{\max}$ (strict inequality), the stationarity condition reduces to $\nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_\mathrm{ret} = \mathbf{0}$. That is, the retain loss alone governs the parameters, free from any opposing forget gradient.}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textit{Proof.}
(a)~Immediate from the definition: when $H_t \geq \tau H_\mathrm{max}$, the ReLU in~\eqref{eq:clamp_token} outputs zero, so $\ell_t = 0$ is constant in a neighborhood of $\thetav$, and $\nabla_{\thetav} \ell_t = \mathbf{0}$.

(b)~The logit-level gradient is $\nabla_{z_t} H_t = -(\mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^\top)(\log p + \mathbf{1})$.
The softmax Jacobian $J = \mathrm{diag}(p) - pp^\top$ has rank $V\!-\!1$ (its null space is $\mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})$).
Now $\nabla_{z_t} H_t = \mathbf{0}$ iff $J(\log p + \mathbf{1}) = \mathbf{0}$ iff $\log p + \mathbf{1} \in \mathrm{span}(\mathbf{1})$ iff $\log p_v$ is constant for all $v$ iff $p$ is uniform.
Therefore $\nabla_{z_t}(-H_t) \neq \mathbf{0}$ for every non-uniform distribution, confirming that unclamped entropy maximization pushes logits toward the constant-logit configuration $z_{t,v} = c$ for all $v$ --- which produces the uniform distribution.

Note that multiple parameter vectors $\thetav$ may produce uniform logits; the characterization is in logit space, not parameter space.

(c)~If all forget tokens satisfy $H_t > \tau H_\mathrm{max}$ (strict inequality), then by continuity of $H_t(\thetav)$ the strict inequality holds in a neighborhood of $\thetav$, so $\Loss_\mathrm{fgt}(\thetav') = 0$ for all $\thetav'$ in this neighborhood and $\nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_\mathrm{fgt} = \mathbf{0}$.
The stationarity condition $\nabla_{\thetav}(\Loss_\mathrm{fgt} + \lambda \Loss_\mathrm{ret}) = \mathbf{0}$ becomes $\lambda \nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_\mathrm{ret} = \mathbf{0}$, and since $\lambda > 0$, this simplifies to $\nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_\mathrm{ret} = \mathbf{0}$.
Thus the parameters settle at a local minimum of the retain loss, with no opposing force from the forget objective.
\hfill$\square$

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Information-theoretic interpretation.}
The clamping threshold partitions per-token entropy into two regimes: $[0, \tau H_\mathrm{max})$ where unlearning is active, and $[\tau H_\mathrm{max}, H_\mathrm{max}]$ where the forget objective is silent.
With $\tau = 0.7$, forget tokens need only reach $70\%$ of maximum entropy --- uncertain enough that specific memorized content is not recoverable, but far from the uniform distribution that would require flattening all logits.
The remaining $30\%$ of entropy range can be viewed informally as a ``confidence budget'': shared parameters can maintain low-entropy retain predictions without the forget objective pushing them toward uniformity.
This design is well-matched to LLM unlearning, where forget and retain data share most token-level statistics (common words, syntax) and differ only on knowledge-bearing tokens.

\subsection{LoRA Architecture}

We apply LoRA adapters to all seven linear projections in each transformer layer: the four attention projections (\texttt{q\_proj}, \texttt{k\_proj}, \texttt{v\_proj}, \texttt{o\_proj}) and the three feed-forward projections (\texttt{gate\_proj}, \texttt{up\_proj}, \texttt{down\_proj}).
With rank $r = 16$ and scaling $\alpha = 32$ (effective scaling factor $\alpha/r = 2.0$), this yields approximately 160M trainable parameters across 32 layers---roughly 2.3\% of the full 6.7B model.
The base model weights remain frozen; the reference model used for constraint evaluation is simply the base model with LoRA adapters disabled, incurring zero additional memory.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

We evaluate using the standard MUSE evaluation suite.
The five metrics are:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{forget\_knowmem} ($\downarrow$): ROUGE-L F1 on knowledge QA pairs from the forget set (max 32 new tokens). Measures residual factual knowledge.
\item \textbf{verbmem} ($\downarrow$): ROUGE-L F1 on verbatim completion of forget-set passages (max 128 new tokens). Measures residual memorization.
\item \textbf{retain} ($\uparrow$): ROUGE-L F1 on knowledge QA pairs from the retain set. Measures preservation of non-targeted knowledge.
\item \textbf{extract} ($\downarrow$): Extraction strength---for each forget passage, the fraction of tokens the model can reproduce given a prefix. Lower means less recoverable memorization.
\item \textbf{HM} ($\uparrow$): Harmonic mean of $(1 - \text{forget\_knowmem})$, $(1 - \text{verbmem})$, and retain. Penalizes imbalance across axes; any weak dimension tanks the score.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compute}

All experiments are run on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU (80GB).
Training times are wall-clock (excluding evaluation).
\method with clamped entropy on MUSE Books (4 epochs, 135 outer steps) takes approximately 104 minutes.
Gradient checkpointing is enabled for all methods to fit within GPU memory.

\paragraph{PDU retain instability.}
Across all settings, PDU's retain loss exhibits a characteristic pattern: an initial spike followed by persistent oscillations that never fully resolve (\figref{fig:dynamics_extended}b,d). On forget05, PDU's retain loss fluctuates between 0.4 and 1.3 throughout training. On forget10, the oscillations are even larger (0.0--1.6). This instability arises because PDU's primal-dual updates are symmetric---the dual variable drops as quickly as it rises, providing no lasting protection after a retain violation. In contrast, \method's asymmetric 10$\times$ slower decay permanently records the severity of past violations.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{app:ablation}

\paragraph{Forget loss comparison.}
\tabref{tab:ablation_loss} compares forget loss variants within the \method framework on MUSE Books, all using the same bilevel ALM setup ($K = 3$, $\varepsilon = 0.15$, $\lambda_0 = 1.0$, $\rho = 0.1$).
Each loss produces a qualitatively distinct training trajectory:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Logit margin:} Strong forgetting but unbounded gradients cause structural retain spikes at every epoch boundary. The ALM cannot fully recover because each spike's magnitude exceeds what K=3 inner steps can repair. Best retain $\approx 0.41$.
\item \textbf{Repr.\ orthogonal:} Completely stable retain (no spikes ever trigger) but weak forgetting---orthogonalizing hidden states does not suppress token-level generation.
\item \textbf{Clamped entropy:} One spike that permanently ratchets $\lambda$, then smooth convergence. The bounded nature of the loss means subsequent outer steps are predictably sized, allowing the inner loop to fully repair retain damage. Best on both axes.
\end{itemize}

Key findings from the ablation:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A16 (ALM off):} The model collapses at step~31 ($L_\text{ret}$=10.56 triggered safety break). Pure clamped entropy without constraint enforcement destroys the model. The ALM is the core stabilizing mechanism.
\item \textbf{A15 (No LoRA):} Full fine-tuning achieves aggressive forgetting (fk=0.002) but catastrophic retain collapse (ret=0.306, $-$0.352). The low-rank bottleneck provides essential implicit regularization that the bilevel structure alone cannot replace.
\item \textbf{A9 ($\tau$=1.0):} Without clamping, gradients continue pushing already-forgotten tokens, causing 8.2\% retain degradation (0.658$\to$0.604). The per-token stopping criterion is essential for protecting shared representations.
\item \textbf{A1 ($K$=0):} Modest retain drop ($-$0.027). The ALM constraint alone provides strong protection, but the inner loop adds a safety margin by pre-repairing retain before each outer step. Training is 2.5$\times$ faster per step without the inner loop. \figref{fig:inner_loop_dynamics} visualizes the mechanism: during the phase-transition spike (steps 28--40), the inner loop fires 18 emergency SGD steps that pull $L_\text{ret}$ down faster, resulting in 1.3 less cumulative constraint violation (17.5 vs.\ 18.8). This 7\% violation reduction translates directly to the 0.027 retain gap at convergence.
\item \textbf{A18 (Swapped):} Putting forgetting in the inner loop causes $\lambda$ to explode to 367 because the constraint threshold $\varepsilon_\text{fgt}$=22.4 exceeds clamped entropy's theoretical maximum ($\tau \cdot H_\text{max} \approx 8.3$)---physically impossible to satisfy.
\item \textbf{A4/A17 (Unbounded losses):} GA produces $L_\text{fgt}$=$-$7.2 (unbounded negative) and collapses retain; logit margin destroys all representations even with full ALM protection. Both confirm that bounded forget losses are essential for bilevel stability.
\item \textbf{A5 (NPO):} The NPO loss collapses to 0.0 by step~40 without actually unlearning---verbatim memorization remains at 0.997. The model satisfies NPO trivially because long-sequence probabilities under the reference model are already low, regardless of memorization.
\end{itemize}

\section{Training Dynamics Analysis}
\label{app:dynamics_analysis}

We analyze the loss curve behavior of \method and PDU across benchmarks and identify structural patterns that explain the performance differences observed in Tables~\ref{tab:tofu_1b_full}--\ref{tab:sust_scal}.

\subsection{Three-Phase Dynamics of \method}

Across all benchmarks and forget set sizes, \method exhibits a universal three-phase training pattern:

\paragraph{Phase 1: Warm-up (steps 1--$T_\text{trans}$).}
The forget loss decreases steadily from its initial value (${\sim}7.0$ for MUSE Books) while retain loss stays near $\varepsilon$. The dual variable $\lambda$ remains approximately constant at its initial value ($\lambda_0 = 1.0$). During this phase, the clamped entropy objective is ``easy''---most tokens have low entropy and contribute gradient---so the outer loop makes rapid progress without disturbing retain.

\paragraph{Phase 2: Spike-and-ratchet ($T_\text{trans}$ to $T_\text{trans} + {\sim}15$ steps).}
At a critical transition point $T_\text{trans}$, the model encounters a phase transition where the forget loss drops sharply but temporarily destabilizes retain. The retain loss spikes (e.g., from 0.15 to 1.65 on MUSE Books), triggering the asymmetric dual update which rapidly ratchets $\lambda$ upward ($1.0 \to 2.27$ on Books). The inner loop fires $K$ emergency SGD steps per outer step to repair retain damage. This phase is brief but critical: it permanently calibrates $\lambda$ to the task difficulty.

\paragraph{Phase 3: Smooth convergence ($T_\text{trans} + 15$ to end).}
After the ratchet, the elevated $\lambda$ provides sufficient retain protection. The forget loss continues to decrease but with a shrinking effective gradient (fewer tokens remain ``active'' above the clamp threshold). Retain loss stays stably below $\varepsilon$. The $\lambda$ value slowly decays by the asymmetric factor ($0.1\rho$ per step of satisfaction) but never returns to pre-ratchet levels.

\paragraph{Stability of $T_\text{trans}$.}
The transition point $T_\text{trans} \approx 28$ steps is remarkably stable across benchmarks: 28 on MUSE Books, 30 on TOFU 1B fgt01, 28 on TOFU 1B fgt05. This stability arises because $T_\text{trans}$ is determined by when the LoRA adapter has accumulated enough low-rank perturbation to qualitatively change the output distribution on forget tokens---a property of the LoRA parameterization rather than the dataset.

\subsection{Adaptive $\lambda$ as Difficulty Diagnostic}

The final $\lambda$ value after the ratchet phase scales monotonically with task difficulty:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item TOFU 1B fgt01 (20 QA pairs): $\lambda_\text{final} \approx 2.4$
\item TOFU 1B fgt05 (100 QA pairs): $\lambda_\text{final} \approx 3.6$
\item TOFU 1B fgt10 (200 QA pairs): $\lambda_\text{final} \approx 4.5$
\item MUSE Books (554 documents): $\lambda_\text{final} \approx 2.3$
\end{itemize}
Larger forget sets require more aggressive forgetting, which causes larger retain spikes, which in turn ratchet $\lambda$ higher. This automatic scaling requires no hyperparameter adjustment between settings---the same initial $\lambda_0 = 1.0$ and $\rho = 0.1$ produce appropriate constraint enforcement across a 10$\times$ range of forget set sizes.

\paragraph{$\lambda$ plateau as convergence diagnostic.}
Once $\lambda$ reaches its plateau (visible as a flat segment in the dynamics plots), the system has found a stable balance between forgetting and retention. This provides a \emph{built-in convergence diagnostic} without requiring held-out validation: if $\lambda$ is still rising, the model has not yet found an equilibrium; if $\lambda$ is flat, training can be safely stopped. In practice, $\lambda$ plateaus 10--15 steps after the spike, coinciding with $\Loss_\text{fgt}$ dropping below 1.0.

\subsection{PDU Instability: Oscillatory Limit Cycles}

PDU \citep{entesari2025constrained} uses an unbounded logit-margin loss with symmetric primal-dual updates. Our dynamics analysis reveals a structural instability:

\paragraph{Unbounded loss $\Rightarrow$ divergent gradients.}
PDU's forget loss (logit margin) has no upper bound. As training progresses and the model becomes more confident on forget tokens, the margin loss grows, producing increasingly large outer perturbations. Unlike \method's clamped entropy (bounded by $\tau \cdot H_\text{max}$), there is no mechanism to attenuate the forget gradient as forgetting succeeds.

\paragraph{Symmetric dual $\Rightarrow$ no ratchet.}
PDU's dual variable uses symmetric updates: it rises during violation and falls equally fast during satisfaction. After a retain spike ratchets the dual up, subsequent satisfaction immediately erodes the protection. This creates a limit cycle: large perturbation $\to$ retain spike $\to$ $\lambda$ rises $\to$ retain recovers $\to$ $\lambda$ falls $\to$ large perturbation again.

\paragraph{Empirical signature.}
On MUSE News, PDU's retain loss oscillates between 0.8 and 3.0 throughout training, never achieving the stable sub-$\varepsilon$ convergence that \method exhibits after its single ratchet event. On MUSE Books, PDU shows 5+ distinct retain spikes across 4 epochs, each partially repaired but never fully resolved. These oscillations directly cause the worse HM scores: each spike damages shared representations, and while the token-level metrics may recover, the generation quality (measured by LLM judge) does not fully bounce back.

\paragraph{Implication for scalability.}
The oscillatory instability compounds under repeated application (sustainability) and larger forget sets (scalability). Each sequential unlearning step starts from a model that has accumulated representational damage from prior oscillations. By step~3, PDU's retain loss diverges entirely (HM=0.016). \method's monotonic $\lambda$ ratchet prevents this accumulation: each application starts from a model where $\lambda$ has permanently encoded the protection level needed.
