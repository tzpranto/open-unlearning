\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We formulate LLM unlearning as constrained bilevel optimization. The inner loop preserves retain performance; the outer loop drives forgetting subject to an augmented Lagrangian constraint.
All updates are confined to LoRA adapters attached to a frozen base model.

\subsection{Problem Setup}

Let $M$ be a pretrained LLM with parameters $\thetav_0$, $\Dforget$ the data to unlearn, and $\Dretain$ data the model should preserve.
We seek parameters $\thetav$ such that (i) the model's output distribution on $\Dforget$ is high-entropy (memorized content is forgotten) and (ii) the cross-entropy loss on $\Dretain$ remains below a threshold $\varepsilon$.

\subsection{LoRA Parameterization}
\label{sec:lora}

Rather than modifying full weight matrices, we attach LoRA adapters \citep{hu2022lora} to all projection matrices ($q$/$k$/$v$/$o$\_proj, gate/up/down\_proj):
\begin{equation}
    W' = W_0 + \tfrac{\alpha}{r}\, B A,\quad B \!\in\! \R^{d \times r},\; A \!\in\! \R^{r \times d}
    \label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
where $r \ll d$ is the rank and $\alpha/r$ is a scaling factor.
With $r\!=\!16$ on a 7B model, only ${\sim}0.08\%$ of parameters are trainable.
This serves two purposes for unlearning: (a) the low-rank subspace limits the expressivity of the update, preventing catastrophic weight drift, and (b) disabling adapters recovers the exact original model, simplifying debugging and rollback.

\subsection{Bilevel Formulation}
\label{sec:bilevel}

We decompose the problem into two nested optimization levels: \emph{retain in the inner loop, forget in the outer loop}.

\paragraph{Inner loop (retain preservation).}
For each outer step, run $K$ SGD steps on the retain set:
\begin{equation}
    \thetav \leftarrow \thetav - \eta_{\text{in}} \nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_{\text{CE}}(M_{\thetav}; \mathcal{B}_{\text{ret}})
    \label{eq:inner}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_{\text{ret}} \sim \Dretain$ is a mini-batch and $\Loss_{\text{CE}}$ is cross-entropy.
The inner loop uses a fast learning rate ($\eta_{\text{in}} = 2\text{e-}4$) to restore any retain damage from the previous outer step.

\paragraph{Outer loop (constrained forgetting).}
The outer objective combines the forget loss with an inequality constraint on retain performance, enforced via an augmented Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
    \Loss_{\text{outer}} = \Loss_{\text{fgt}} + \lambda\, r + \tfrac{\rho}{2}[\max(0,r)]^2,\; r = L_{\text{ret}} {-} \varepsilon
    \label{eq:outer}
\end{equation}
where $L_{\text{ret}} = \Loss_{\text{CE}}(M_{\thetav}; \mathcal{B}'_{\text{ret}})$ is evaluated on a \emph{fresh} retain batch (separate from the inner loop), $\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier, $\rho$ is the quadratic penalty coefficient, and $\varepsilon$ is the retain budget.
The outer loop uses a slow learning rate ($\eta_{\text{out}} = 3\text{e-}5$), keeping each forgetting step small enough for the inner loop to repair.

\paragraph{Repair-first principle.}
This assignment inverts prior bilevel unlearning methods: SCRUB \citep{kurmanji2023towards} and BLUR \citep{reisizadeh2026blur} put forgetting in the inner loop and retain repair in the outer loop. Our reasoning is different.

Forgetting is inherently destructive. Gradient ascent, entropy maximization, and preference-based losses all push the model \emph{away} from learned representations, and such updates cause catastrophic collapse when applied without constraint \citep{zhang2024negative}. The problem is compounded by the fact that forget and retain knowledge share entangled representations in transformer networks \citep{cheng2026unlearning}---perturbing forget tokens inevitably disturbs retain performance.

Given this, retain repair belongs in the inner loop as a stabilizing anchor. It runs first and fast ($\eta_{\text{in}} / \eta_{\text{out}} \approx 7$--$10\times$), so the model enters each outer step from a state with good retain performance. The outer loop then applies one small forgetting step---sized so the next inner loop can fully repair any collateral damage. This prevents the accumulation of representational damage that makes repair increasingly difficult when the loops are reversed.

\subsection{Clamped Entropy Loss}
\label{sec:clamped_entropy}

Entropy maximization as a forget objective is well-known \citep{yuan2025closer}, but unclamped formulations apply nonzero gradients to every token at every step, even those whose distribution is already near-uniform. This produces unnecessarily large perturbations that destabilize bilevel optimization.
We introduce \emph{clamped entropy}, which defines when a token is ``sufficiently forgotten'' and stops optimizing it:
\begin{equation}
    \Loss_{\text{forget}} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \max\!\big(0,\; \tau \cdot H_{\max} - H(p_t)\big)
    \label{eq:clamped_entropy}
\end{equation}
where $p_t = \text{softmax}(z_t)$ is the output distribution at position $t$, $H(p_t) = -\sum_v p_t(v) \log p_t(v)$ is its Shannon entropy, $H_{\max} = \log V$ is the entropy of a uniform distribution over vocabulary $V$, and $\tau \in (0,1)$ is the target fraction.

\paragraph{Vocabulary-invariant threshold.}
Normalizing by $H_{\max} = \log V$ makes $\tau$ interpretable across models: $\tau = 0.7$ means ``70\% of maximum possible uncertainty,'' whether the vocabulary has 32K tokens (Llama-2) or 128K tokens (Llama-3). Without this, a fixed entropy threshold would need re-tuning for each tokenizer.

\paragraph{Hard deadzone.}
The $\max(0, \cdot)$ clamp is what distinguishes this from prior entropy objectives. Once $H(p_t) \geq \tau \cdot H_{\max}$, the gradient contribution of token $t$ is \emph{exactly zero}---not approximately small, but identically zero. Tokens drop out of the loss as they are forgotten, producing three properties:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, topsep=2pt, itemsep=1pt]
    \item \textbf{Bounded loss}: $\Loss_{\text{forget}} \in [0, \tau \cdot H_{\max}]$. The outer perturbation magnitude is bounded by construction (Proposition~1), so the inner loop's $K$ steps can reliably repair retain damage regardless of training stage.
    \item \textbf{Self-stabilizing}: the number of active tokens (those with $H(p_t) < \tau \cdot H_{\max}$) shrinks monotonically during training. The effective gradient norm decreases automatically, decelerating the outer loop without scheduling.
    \item \textbf{Reference-free}: requires only a single forward pass, unlike DPO-style losses (NPO, SimNPO) that need a frozen reference model in memory.
\end{itemize}

\noindent By contrast, unclamped entropy maximization \citep{yuan2025closer} applies gradient to \emph{all} tokens at \emph{all} steps---including those already at 99\% of $H_{\max}$---wasting budget and creating unnecessary perturbations to shared representations. Gradient ascent \citep{jang2023knowledge} is worse still: its loss is unbounded ($-\log p \to \infty$ as $p \to 0$), so a single highly-memorized token can dominate the gradient (Proposition~1c).

\subsection{Augmented Lagrangian with Asymmetric Dual Update}
\label{sec:alm}

The multiplier $\lambda$ controls how much weight retain preservation receives in the outer objective.
Rather than treating it as a fixed hyperparameter, we learn it via a dual update rule.

Standard ALM for equality constraints uses symmetric updates and a penalty that is always active \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear}.
We enforce an \emph{inequality} constraint $L_{\text{ret}} \leq \varepsilon$: the quadratic penalty $\frac{\rho}{2}[\max(0, r)]^2$ activates only when violated (Eq.~\ref{eq:outer}).
The model is never penalized for achieving retain loss below $\varepsilon$---it is free to improve on retain without opposition.
On top of this, we use an \emph{asymmetric} dual update:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda \leftarrow \begin{cases}
        \lambda + \rho \cdot r & \text{if } r > 0 \;\;\text{(violation)} \\
        \lambda + \alpha\rho \cdot r & \text{if } r \leq 0 \;\;\text{(satisfaction)}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:dual_update}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha = 0.1$. The $10\times$ slower decay means that once a retain violation drives $\lambda$ up, it stays elevated. Without this asymmetry, $\lambda$ drops back between spikes, causing repeated forget--retain oscillation cycles (as we observe empirically with PDU).

\paragraph{Auto-epsilon.}
Rather than hand-tuning an absolute $\varepsilon$, we set it as a fraction of the model's initial retain loss. Before training, we run one inner loop and compute:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon = \varepsilon_{\text{mul}} \cdot \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K} \Loss_{\text{CE}}^{(k)}
    \label{eq:auto_eps}
\end{equation}
where $\Loss_{\text{CE}}^{(k)}$ is the retain loss at inner step $k$. This adapts $\varepsilon$ automatically to the model and dataset.

\paragraph{Convergence criterion.}
Training terminates when $\lambda$ plateaus: $|\lambda_t - \lambda_{t-w}| < \delta$ over a window of $w$ outer steps.
At this point the constraint is satisfied ($r \leq 0$) and the forget loss contributes negligible gradient (most tokens have entered the deadzone).
This gives a stopping rule without held-out validation: a rising $\lambda$ signals ongoing conflict, while a flat $\lambda$ confirms equilibrium.
In practice, $\lambda$ plateaus 10--15 steps after the initial ratchet, coinciding with $\Loss_\text{fgt}$ dropping below 1.0.

\subsection{Algorithm Summary}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\method: Bilevel Augmented Lagrangian Unlearning}
\label{alg:lora_bial}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Model $M$ with frozen weights $\thetav_0$, $\Dforget$, $\Dretain$
\REQUIRE Hyperparams: $K$, $\eta_{\text{in}}$, $\eta_{\text{out}}$, $\varepsilon_{\text{mul}}$, $\rho$, $\lambda_0$, $\alpha$, $\tau$, $T$
\STATE Attach LoRA adapters to $M$; $\thetav \leftarrow$ LoRA params
\STATE $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda_0$
\STATE $\varepsilon \leftarrow \varepsilon_{\text{mul}} \cdot \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \Loss_{\text{CE}}^{(k)}(\Dretain)$
\FOR{$t = 1$ \TO $T$}
    \STATE \textbf{Inner loop} (retain repair):
    \FOR{$k = 1$ \TO $K$}
        \STATE $\thetav \leftarrow \thetav - \eta_{\text{in}} \nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_{\text{CE}}(M_{\thetav}; \mathcal{B}_{\text{ret}})$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE \textbf{Forget loss} (clamped entropy):
    \STATE $\Loss_{\text{fgt}} \leftarrow \frac{1}{T}\sum_t \max(0,\; \tau \log V - H(p_t))$
    \STATE \textbf{Outer loss} (augmented Lagrangian):
    \STATE $r \leftarrow \Loss_{\text{CE}}(M_{\thetav}; \mathcal{B}'_{\text{ret}}) - \varepsilon$
    \STATE $\Loss_{\text{outer}} \leftarrow \Loss_{\text{fgt}} + \lambda\, r + \frac{\rho}{2}[\max(0, r)]^2$
    \STATE $\thetav \leftarrow \thetav - \eta_{\text{out}} \cdot \text{Adam}(\nabla_{\thetav} \Loss_{\text{outer}})$
    \STATE \textbf{Dual update} (asymmetric ratchet):
    \IF{$r > 0$}
        \STATE $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \rho \cdot r$
    \ELSE
        \STATE $\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \alpha\rho \cdot r$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\RETURN $M$ with trained LoRA adapters
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
