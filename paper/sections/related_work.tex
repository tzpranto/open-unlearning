\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Machine Unlearning for LLMs}

The concept of machine unlearning was first formalized by \citet{cao2015towards}, who proposed efficient data removal from learned models without full retraining. \citet{bourtoule2021machine} later introduced SISA training, which partitions data during training to make unlearning tractable. However, these approaches are designed for classification models and do not easily extend to large autoregressive language models.

For LLMs specifically, gradient ascent (GA) is the most straightforward approach: simply maximize the loss on the forget set to push the model away from memorized content \citep{jang2023knowledge}. While conceptually simple, GA suffers from severe utility degradation --- the model quickly loses coherence on all inputs, not just the targeted data. \citet{yao2023large} proposed GradDiff, which combines gradient ascent on forget data with standard cross-entropy on retain data. This helps balance the two objectives but the method is still quite unstable in practice, and the retain performance often degrades significantly as the forget loss keeps pushing model weights in destructive directions.

A different line of work draws inspiration from preference optimization. \citet{zhang2024negative} proposed NPO, which adapts the DPO framework \citep{rafailov2023direct} by treating forget samples as ``negative preferences'' without requiring positive counterparts. They show theoretically that the catastrophic collapse under NPO is exponentially slower compared to GA, which is a nice result. However, NPO still requires maintaining a frozen reference model in memory, which is not ideal for 7B+ parameter models where GPU memory is already tight. \citet{fan2024simplicity} address this with SimNPO, dropping the reference model entirely and showing competitive results on TOFU and MUSE. More recently, \citet{bu2024unlearning} frame unlearning as multi-task optimization and propose NGDiff which normalizes the gradient difference between forget and retain objectives. This is motivated by the observation that the magnitudes of the two gradient directions are often very different, causing the naive combination to be dominated by one objective.

Taking a representation-level approach, \citet{li2024wmdp} proposed RMU (Representation Mismatch Unlearning) as part of their WMDP benchmark for measuring hazardous knowledge. Instead of operating on the loss landscape, RMU steers intermediate representations toward random target vectors for forget data while anchoring retain representations. This is an interesting direction but it does not come with the same kind of formal guarantees as optimization-based methods.

Several works have explored more creative strategies. \citet{eldan2023whos} demonstrated that Harry Potter knowledge can be approximately removed from Llama-2-7b by generating substitute labels where distinctive terms are replaced with generic alternatives. While this shows impressive qualitative results, the approach is highly task-specific and requires substantial manual effort for each new unlearning target. \citet{kurmanji2023towards} proposed SCRUB, a teacher-student framework for unlearning that achieves strong forget quality across multiple applications but is designed primarily for smaller classification models.

A common failure mode across these methods is the fundamental tension between forgetting and retention: pushing harder on forget data inevitably damages retain performance. Most existing approaches try to balance these objectives through loss weighting or gradient manipulation, but they lack a principled framework for enforcing the retain constraint. Our work addresses exactly this gap by formulating unlearning as a constrained bilevel optimization problem.

\subsection{Unlearning Benchmarks and Evaluation}

Evaluating unlearning is arguably as challenging as the unlearning itself. \citet{maini2024tofu} introduced TOFU, a benchmark built around 200 synthetic author biographies with associated QA pairs. The key idea is that since the data is fictitious, a gold standard ``retrained'' model naturally exists (one that was never trained on the forget set). They evaluate through truth ratios, ROUGE scores, and membership inference attacks, providing a multifaceted view of unlearning quality. Importantly, they found that none of the baselines they tested achieved truly effective unlearning, highlighting the difficulty of the problem.

\citet{shi2024muse} proposed MUSE, which goes further by defining six desirable properties for an unlearned model: no verbatim memorization, no knowledge memorization, no privacy leakage, utility preservation, scalability, and sustainability. They evaluate on two realistic corpora --- news articles and Harry Potter books --- with Llama-2-7b as the base model. Their evaluation reveals that while some methods reduce memorization, most fail on privacy leakage or severely degrade utility. The WMDP benchmark \citep{li2024wmdp} takes a safety-oriented perspective, measuring hazardous knowledge in biosecurity and cybersecurity domains with multiple-choice questions.

We primarily evaluate on both TOFU and MUSE benchmarks, as they provide complementary views: TOFU tests unlearning of factual associations in a controlled setting, while MUSE tests removal of naturalistic memorized content at larger scale.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Methods and Weight Surgery}

The idea of editing model behavior through weight-space operations has gained traction recently. \citet{ilharco2023editing} showed that task vectors --- the difference between fine-tuned and pretrained weights --- support arithmetic operations, and negating a task vector effectively reduces the associated capability. This insight is directly relevant to unlearning, as one can subtract the ``forget task vector'' from the model weights.

LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} has become the de facto method for parameter-efficient adaptation of LLMs, factorizing weight updates into low-rank matrices that dramatically reduce trainable parameters and memory requirements. Several recent works have explored LoRA specifically for unlearning. \citet{cha2024loku} proposed LoKU, which addresses the poor trade-off between forgetting and retention when naively combining GA with LoRA. They introduce an inverted hinge loss and Fisher-informed LoRA initialization to improve this trade-off. \citet{jia2024wagle} proposed WAGLE, which attributes importance scores to model weights and applies strategic masking for modular unlearning.

Our approach combines weight surgery (via Fisher-weighted task vector negation in the initialization stage) with LoRA-based bilevel fine-tuning. The key difference from prior parameter-efficient approaches is that we use LoRA not just for memory efficiency but also as a structural constraint --- the low-rank bottleneck naturally limits the expressivity of the unlearning update, preventing the kind of catastrophic weight changes that plague full-parameter methods.

\subsection{Bilevel Optimization}

Bilevel optimization has a long history in operations research \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear} and has seen growing adoption in machine learning. The most well-known example is MAML \citep{finn2017model}, where the outer loop optimizes for fast adaptation while the inner loop performs task-specific fine-tuning. \citet{franceschi2018bilevel} provided a unified bilevel programming framework for hyperparameter optimization and meta-learning. Computing exact hypergradients requires second-order information which is expensive for large models, so various approximations have been proposed: implicit differentiation \citep{lorraine2020optimizing}, Neumann series expansion for inverse Hessian approximation \citep{ji2021bilevel}, and first-order approaches that avoid Hessian computation entirely.

The augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear, nocedal2006numerical} is a classical approach for constrained optimization that maintains dual variables (Lagrange multipliers) alongside the primal objective. Unlike simple penalty methods that require driving the penalty coefficient to infinity, ALM achieves exact constraint satisfaction with finite penalty parameters through adaptive dual variable updates. This property makes it well-suited for our setting where we need to strictly enforce the retain performance constraint.

The most closely related work to ours is BLUR \citep{reisizadeh2025blur}, which also formulates LLM unlearning as bilevel optimization. However, there are significant differences in formulation and implementation. BLUR uses the retain loss as the lower-level objective and the forget loss as the upper-level one, optimizing through implicit differentiation. Our approach instead uses an augmented Lagrangian formulation that treats retain performance as an explicit constraint with adaptive enforcement, and we further combine this with LoRA adapters and Fisher-based initialization --- a three-stage pipeline that achieves competitive results in under 3 minutes compared to over an hour for most existing methods. \citet{jia2024soul} also explored second-order optimization for unlearning through Newton-step approximations via influence functions \citep{koh2017understanding}, providing another perspective on how curvature information can benefit the unlearning process.

Finally, we note that there is an interesting connection between machine unlearning and catastrophic forgetting in continual learning \citep{kirkpatrick2017overcoming}. Both problems involve selectively managing what a neural network remembers, though with opposite goals --- continual learning tries to prevent forgetting while unlearning tries to induce it. The Fisher information matrix plays a central role in both: EWC \citep{kirkpatrick2017overcoming} uses it to identify parameters important for old tasks (to protect them), while Fisher-based unlearning methods \citep{golatkar2020eternal, golatkar2020forgetting} use it to identify parameters important for the forget set (to modify them). Our PerTA initialization stage exploits exactly this duality.
