\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Machine Unlearning for LLMs}

Machine unlearning was formalized by \citet{cao2015towards} for efficient data removal without full retraining. \citet{bourtoule2021machine} introduced SISA training, partitioning data to make unlearning tractable, but these approaches target classification and do not transfer to autoregressive language models.

For LLMs, gradient ascent (GA) is the most direct approach: maximize the loss on the forget set \citep{jang2023knowledge}. The loss is unbounded, grows without limit, and rapidly destroys coherence. GradDiff \citep{yao2024large} combines GA with cross-entropy on retain data, but retain performance still degrades because the forget gradients push weights in destructive directions.

A preference-optimization line began with NPO \citep{zhang2024negative}, adapting DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct} by treating forget samples as negative preferences. NPO collapses more slowly than GA but requires a reference model and saturates without converging to a well-defined target. SimNPO \citep{fan2025simplicity} drops the reference model while remaining competitive. NGDiff \citep{bu2025unlearning} normalizes the gradient difference between forget and retain so that neither dominates.

At the representation level, RMU \citep{li2024wmdp} steers intermediate activations toward random targets for forget data while anchoring retain representations. It is effective in its benchmark but lacks the optimization-theoretic grounding of loss-based methods.

What these approaches share is the absence of a \emph{bounded} forget objective with a per-token stopping criterion. GA diverges. NPO damps asymptotically but never reaches zero. Unclamped entropy maximization \citep{yuan2025closer} pushes all tokens toward uniform indefinitely. The inverted hinge of LoKU \citep{cha2025loku} is bounded but operates on token margins, not distributional entropy, and offers no tunable threshold. Our clamped entropy loss defines a vocabulary-invariant sufficiency threshold and produces exactly zero gradient once a token crosses it.

\subsection{Unlearning Benchmarks}

\citet{maini2024tofu} introduced TOFU, built around 200 synthetic author biographies with QA pairs and a gold retrained model. They evaluate via truth ratios, ROUGE, and membership inference. \citet{shi2025muse} proposed MUSE, defining six properties (no verbatim/knowledge memorization, no privacy leakage, utility preservation, scalability, sustainability) over two corpora (news articles and Harry Potter) with Llama-2-7b. KnowUndo \citep{tian2024knowundo} targets copyright and privacy unlearning from LoRA-fine-tuned models.

We evaluate on TOFU, MUSE, and KnowUndo because they provide complementary views: TOFU tests factual associations in a controlled setting, MUSE tests naturalistic memorization at scale with sustainability/scalability stress tests, and KnowUndo tests domain-specific unlearning from adapter-tuned models.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Unlearning}

LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} factorizes weight updates into low-rank matrices, reducing trainable parameters by orders of magnitude. Several works apply LoRA to unlearning: LoKU \citep{cha2025loku} introduces Fisher-informed LoRA initialization with an inverted hinge loss, and WAGLE \citep{jia2024wagle} assigns importance scores for strategic weight masking.

In \method, LoRA serves as a \emph{structural constraint}: the low-rank bottleneck limits how much the unlearning update can change the model, preventing catastrophic drift that occurs with full-parameter methods. The inner loop protects retain within this small subspace, while the outer loop drives forgetting through the same bottleneck.

\subsection{Bilevel and Constrained Optimization}

Bilevel optimization has a long history in operations research \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear} and is increasingly used in machine learning via MAML \citep{finn2017model} and hyperparameter optimization \citep{franceschi2018bilevel}. The augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear, nocedal2006numerical} maintains dual variables for constrained optimization and can achieve exact constraint satisfaction with finite parameters through adaptive dual updates.

Three recent works apply constrained or bilevel optimization to unlearning. BLUR \citep{reisizadeh2026blur} uses retain as the lower-level objective and forget as the upper-level, optimizing through implicit differentiation. PDU \citep{entesari2025constrained} uses a linear Lagrangian (no quadratic penalty) with a logit-margin forget loss and symmetric dual updates. \citet{cheng2026unlearning} use an augmented Lagrangian with an equality constraint, a flat single-level optimizer (not bilevel), and evaluation only on vision models.

\method differs structurally: (a) we enforce an \emph{inequality} constraint---the model is never penalized for retain loss below $\varepsilon$, unlike equality formulations; (b) the dual update is \emph{asymmetric} (fast increase on violation, $10\times$ slower decay), producing one-shot ratchet behavior rather than recurring oscillations; (c) retain is in the \emph{inner loop}, creating a repair-first dynamic that inverts SCRUB and BLUR; and (d) the forget objective is clamped entropy---bounded with a hard per-token deadzone---unlike PDU's unbounded logit margin or the gradient ascent in BLUR. \citet{jia2024soul} explored second-order unlearning through influence functions \citep{koh2017understanding}, but without bilevel structure or constraint enforcement.
