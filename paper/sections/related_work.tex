\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Machine Unlearning for LLMs}

The concept of machine unlearning was first formalized by \citet{cao2015towards}, who proposed efficient data removal from learned models without full retraining. \citet{bourtoule2021machine} later introduced SISA training, which partitions data during training to make unlearning tractable. However, these approaches target classification models and do not extend to large autoregressive language models.

For LLMs, gradient ascent (GA) is the most direct approach: maximize the loss on the forget set to push the model away from memorized content \citep{jang2023knowledge}. GA is unbounded---the loss grows without limit, rapidly destroying model coherence on all inputs. \citet{yao2023large} proposed GradDiff, combining gradient ascent on forget data with cross-entropy on retain data, but the retain performance still degrades as the forget loss drives weights in destructive directions.

A preference-optimization line of work began with NPO \citep{zhang2024negative}, which adapts DPO \citep{rafailov2023direct} by treating forget samples as negative preferences. NPO's collapse is exponentially slower than GA's, but it requires a frozen reference model in memory and its objective saturates rather than converging to a well-defined target. \citet{fan2024simplicity} address the reference-model cost with SimNPO, dropping it entirely while remaining competitive on TOFU and MUSE. \citet{bu2024unlearning} take a multi-task view with NGDiff, normalizing the gradient difference between forget and retain objectives to prevent one from dominating.

At the representation level, \citet{li2024wmdp} proposed RMU as part of the WMDP benchmark, steering intermediate representations toward random targets for forget data while anchoring retain representations. While effective in their benchmark, RMU lacks the optimization-theoretic grounding of loss-based methods.

A common thread across these approaches is the absence of a \emph{bounded, self-stabilizing} forget objective. GA is unbounded and diverges. NPO saturates but offers no explicit stopping criterion. Na\"ive entropy maximization pushes all tokens toward the uniform distribution indefinitely, well past the point of effective forgetting. Our clamped entropy loss addresses this directly: it maximizes per-token entropy only up to a threshold, after which the gradient vanishes, providing a natural stopping criterion that prevents over-forgetting.

\subsection{Unlearning Benchmarks and Evaluation}

Evaluating unlearning is arguably as challenging as the unlearning itself. \citet{maini2024tofu} introduced TOFU, a benchmark built around 200 synthetic author biographies with associated QA pairs. Since the data is fictitious, a gold-standard retrained model naturally exists. They evaluate through truth ratios, ROUGE scores, and membership inference attacks, and find that no tested baseline achieves truly effective unlearning.

\citet{shi2024muse} proposed MUSE, which defines six desirable properties for an unlearned model: no verbatim memorization, no knowledge memorization, no privacy leakage, utility preservation, scalability, and sustainability. They evaluate on two realistic corpora---news articles and Harry Potter books---with Llama-2-7b as the base model. Their evaluation reveals that while some methods reduce memorization, most fail on privacy leakage or severely degrade utility. The WMDP benchmark \citep{li2024wmdp} takes a safety-oriented perspective, measuring hazardous knowledge in biosecurity and cybersecurity domains.

We evaluate on both TOFU and MUSE, as they provide complementary views: TOFU tests unlearning of factual associations in a controlled setting, while MUSE tests removal of naturalistic memorized content at larger scale.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Unlearning}

LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} has become the standard method for parameter-efficient adaptation of LLMs, factorizing weight updates into low-rank matrices that reduce trainable parameters and memory requirements by orders of magnitude. Several recent works apply LoRA specifically to unlearning. \citet{cha2024loku} proposed LoKU, which addresses the poor forget--retain trade-off when combining GA with LoRA by introducing an inverted hinge loss and Fisher-informed LoRA initialization. \citet{jia2024wagle} proposed WAGLE, which attributes importance scores to model weights and applies strategic masking for modular unlearning.

In \method, we use LoRA not merely for memory efficiency but as a \emph{structural constraint}: the low-rank bottleneck limits the expressivity of the unlearning update, preventing the catastrophic weight changes that plague full-parameter methods. This complements our bilevel formulation---the inner loop protects retain performance within a small parameter subspace, while the outer loop drives forgetting through the same bottleneck.

\subsection{Bilevel Optimization}

Bilevel optimization has a long history in operations research \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear} and growing adoption in machine learning. MAML \citep{finn2017model} optimizes for fast adaptation in the outer loop while performing task-specific fine-tuning in the inner loop. \citet{franceschi2018bilevel} provided a unified bilevel framework for hyperparameter optimization and meta-learning. Computing exact hypergradients requires second-order information, so various approximations have been proposed: implicit differentiation \citep{lorraine2020optimizing}, Neumann series for inverse Hessian approximation \citep{ji2021bilevel}, and first-order approaches that avoid Hessian computation entirely.

The augmented Lagrangian method (ALM) \citep{bertsekas1999nonlinear, nocedal2006numerical} maintains dual variables alongside the primal objective for constrained optimization. Unlike penalty methods that require driving the penalty to infinity, ALM achieves exact constraint satisfaction with finite parameters through adaptive dual updates. This makes ALM well-suited for our setting, where retain performance must be strictly enforced as a constraint rather than loosely balanced as a loss term.

The most closely related work is BLUR \citep{reisizadeh2025blur}, which also formulates LLM unlearning as bilevel optimization. BLUR uses the retain loss as the lower-level objective and the forget loss as the upper-level one, optimizing through implicit differentiation. Our approach differs in three respects: (a) we use an ALM formulation with an \emph{asymmetric} dual update---fast $\lambda$ increase on constraint violation, slow decrease when satisfied---which produces one-shot ratchet behavior that prevents retain degradation; (b) our forget objective is clamped entropy, a bounded loss that self-stabilizes rather than requiring external stopping criteria; and (c) the LoRA bottleneck constrains the update subspace, reducing the bilevel problem's effective dimensionality. \citet{jia2024soul} also explored second-order optimization for unlearning through Newton-step approximations via influence functions \citep{koh2017understanding}, providing another perspective on curvature-informed unlearning.

We note that our earlier attempt at bilevel unlearning used binary sparsity masks to select which parameters to update, but LoRA's continuous low-rank structure proved both more stable and more effective, motivating the current \method design.
