Paper Summary:
This paper proposed BLADE that combines a clamped entropy forgetting loss, an augmented Lagrangian formulation, and a bilevel optimization framework implemented with LoRA adapters. The method aims to achieve stable unlearning while preserving retained knowledge by alternating retain-repair steps with constrained forgetting updates. Experiments are conducted on three representative LLM unlearning benchmarks (TOFU, MUSE, and KnowUndo), together with additional analyses including adversarial robustness, scalability, sequential unlearning, ablation studies, and training dynamics.

Summary Of Strengths:
This work has a few strenghs:

It addresses an important and timely problem of robust LLM unlearning with an emphasis on preserving retained knowledge.
It tries to propose a bounded clamped-entropy forgetting loss that is intuitively motivated and empirically appears more stable than several existing forgetting objectives.
It conducted extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks, model scales, and evaluation settings, together with comprehensive ablation studies and robustness analyses.

Summary Of Weaknesses:
I believe this work has several weaknesses that limit its overall impact:

The mathematical formulation could be strengthened. The proposed bilevel optimization problem (Eq. (2)) is not always easy to follow, and some key design choices are not sufficiently justified from an optimization perspective. In particular, the Repair-first principle in Section 3.3 is explained mostly through intuitive discussion rather than mathematical analysis. A clearer formulation together with stronger theoretical justification for this design choice would make the method much more convincing.

The paper could benefit from a clearer presentation. For example, the abstract introduces the threshold \tau \cdot \log V without first explaining what \tau or V represent, making the main idea harder to follow upon first reading. Similar issues appear throughout the paper, where notation and optimization concepts are often introduced before they are properly explained. Improving the organization and presentation would make the paper significantly easier to read.

The overall technical novelty appears somewhat less substantial than suggested. While the proposed clamped entropy loss is an interesting idea, several other components, including the LoRA parameterization, augmented Lagrangian optimization, and the alternating retain-repair procedure, are built on existing techniques. The paper would be stronger if it more clearly highlighted its core technical contribution and separated it from the supporting design choices.

Reviewer 2

Paper Summary:
This paper presents a well-structured and carefully designed study of BLADE, with comprehensive experiments across TOFU, MUSE Books, MUSE News, and KnowUndo. The manuscript is clearly organized, appropriately cites related work, and includes interesting adversarial evaluations and extensive empirical examples. However, the relatively weak performance on MUSE News requires further analysis, and the computational overhead introduced by the multiple optimization loops and dual updates should be quantified.

Summary Of Strengths:
The paper is well organized, smoothly written, and provides appropriate coverage of related work.
The experimental design is clean and comprehensive.
The baseline comparison includes a sufficient range of relevant methods, including NPO, RMU, BLURNPO, and PDU.
The experiments cover multiple forgetting ratios on TOFU, including 1%, 5%, and 10%, as well as both the Books and News subsets of MUSE.
The additional adversarial experiments are interesting.
The extensive empirical examples provided in Tables 12–17 are particularly informative.

Summary Of Weaknesses:
The paper does not sufficiently analyze why BLADE performs less favorably on MUSE News.
It remains unclear whether the issue observed on MUSE News is specific to that dataset or may also appear on other datasets not included in the current evaluation.

BLADE appears likely to require greater computational cost than the competing methods because of its multiple optimization loops and dual updates, but the paper does not provide experimental evidence quantifying this overhead.

Comments Suggestions And Typos:
(Major) The paper should provide a more detailed analysis of why BLADE performs relatively poorly on MUSE News. In particular, it would be helpful to identify which characteristics of the dataset or unlearning setting lead to this degradation.

(Major) The authors should investigate whether the limitation observed on MUSE News is specific to that benchmark or also occurs on other datasets. Additional experiments on another dataset would help clarify the generality of this issue.

(Major) Since BLADE involves multiple optimization loops and dual updates, it is likely to incur greater computational cost than the competing methods. Please provide empirical comparisons of training time, memory consumption, or overall computational cost against the main baselines.

Summary Of Weaknesses: The robustness evaluation of the proposed algorithm under the re-learning attack shows that the unlearning is shallow and easy to reverse by fine-tuning on the forget set for just one epoch. The paper lacks evaluation on other common attacks, such as the jailbreak attack, membership inference attack, and optimization-based attacks.
