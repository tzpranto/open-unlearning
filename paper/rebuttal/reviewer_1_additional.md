> Prior two-level unlearning methods like SCRUB (min-max) and BLUR (bilevel with gradient projection) prioritize forgetting at the lower level and treat retain as the upper-level concern. We invert this assignment.
>
> ~~Forgetting is inherently destructive:~~ <span style="color:#0a7">This choice is consistent with two well-documented properties of unlearning:</span> gradient ascent, entropy maximization, and preference-based losses all push the model *away* from learned representations, causing catastrophic collapse when unconstrained (Zhang et al. 2024)<span style="color:#0a7">; and</span> forget and retain knowledge share entangled representations (Cheng et al. 2026), so perturbing forget tokens inevitably disturbs retain performance.
>
> <span style="color:#0a7">From an optimization point of view, a bilevel program is inherently asymmetric: for every one outer step the inner subproblem runs $K>1$ steps, and in our formulation (Eq. 2) the outer objective is additionally constrained on the inner objective's value (retain budget $\varepsilon$). Given that forgetting is destructive, placing it in the unconstrained inner loop would give $K$ unopposed destructive steps between every outer step, a trajectory that a single constrained outer step cannot reliably recover. Repair-first is therefore the natural assignment: retain runs unconstrained in the inner loop, so the model first stabilizes on the shared representation before any forget update is applied.</span>
>
> Retain repair therefore belongs in the inner loop as a stabilizing anchor. It runs first and comparably faster ($\eta_{\text{in}} > \eta_{\text{out}}$), so the model enters each outer step from a state with good retain performance. The outer loop then applies one small forgetting step, sized so the next inner loop can fully repair any collateral damage. This prevents the accumulation of representational drift that makes recovery progressively harder when the loops are reversed. <span style="color:#0a7">The ablation study confirms this: swapping the two levels drops HM from $0.823$ to $0.506$ (Table 3).</span>
>
> For this structure to work, the forget loss in the outer loop must be well-behaved: bounded, self-saturating, and compatible with small step sizes. We introduce *clamped entropy* to address this.
